1.Case Analysis: GDPR in Breast Cancer Research

This assignment aimed to understand the concept and scope of health-related data, as well as its specific legal regime under the GDPR and applicable national legislation. It also sought to identify the legal, ethical and technical aspects to be considered when processing personal health data for research purposes, and to apply the principles and safeguards of the GDPR to the proposed case study, putting forward well-reasoned solutions.

Key conclusions from the analysis

Taking into account the conflict between research needs and patients' right to privacy, these were the main conclusions:

  • Explicit consent: informed consent is the cornerstone. "Broad consent" for future research, however, raises ethical challenges.
  • Anonymization vs. pseudonymization: true anonymization is nearly impossible with genomic data, making robust pseudonymization the only viable legal route.
  • Public interest: cancer research allows for certain exceptions in data processing, always under strict technical and legal safeguards.

2.Essay: Intellectual Property vs. Open Science in Bioinformatics

This work analyzes the ethical and legal dilemma between the need to share bioinformatics data for scientific progress and the protection of intellectual property (patents) to cover research costs and maintain competitive advantage.

Key conclusions from the essay

  • Central ethical conflict: there is a fundamental tension between "open science" (which seeks the common good and medical progress) and "intellectual property" (which seeks to recoup investment and compete).
  • Legal framework (EU): legislation protects databases mainly through sui generis rights. Although raw data itself is in the public domain, interpretations and derived materials may fall under these rights.
  • Proposed solutions: intermediate approaches are suggested, such as "ethical openness", which uses licenses (e.g. Creative Commons) to allow non-commercial scientific and academic use.
